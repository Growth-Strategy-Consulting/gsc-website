Part 01 · 5 prompts

Customer messages and reviews

the inbox that never stops
Answer your top questions
Here are the questions customers ask me most:
[paste your list]
Write a short, friendly answer for each one in my voice. Use our real prices, hours and policies. If you had to guess on any answer, flag it so I can fix it.
Reply to an upset customer
A customer sent me this:
[paste their message]
Write a reply that owns what went wrong in one sentence, offers [what you can do to fix it], and leaves the door open for them to come back. Under 100 words. Give me a calmer version and a warmer version.
Turn a DM into a booking
Someone messaged us: "[their message]"
Write a reply that answers their question in two sentences and ends with our booking link: [link]. Make it sound like a real person, not a bot.
Reply to a review
Here's a [star rating]-star review we got on [Google / Yelp / Facebook]:
[paste review]
Write a reply that thanks them by name, mentions one specific thing they said, and stays under 70 words. If it's negative, own it and invite them to email [your email]. No excuses.
Thank-you text after a visit
Write a short thank-you text I can send the day after someone's [appointment / visit / order]. Mention [what they came in for], ask how it's going, and include our review link: [link]. Under 40 words. Give me three versions so it doesn't feel copy-pasted.

Part 02 · 5 prompts

Social media and promos

say goodbye to blank-page panic
A week of posts from a few notes
Here's what's happening this week: [new items, open spots, events, slow days].
Write 5 posts for [Instagram / Facebook], Monday to Friday. For each one: a hook under 10 words, a caption in my voice, one clear call to action, and a photo idea. No more than two posts should be "buy this."
Fill a slow day
[Day] is always our slowest day. Give me 5 ideas to bring people in on [day] that don't cost much, then write the post for the best one.
Turn a review into a post
Here's a review from a happy customer:
[paste review]
Turn it into a post that tells the story behind it in a few sentences and ends with how to book. Keep their words in quotes and don't change them.
Plan the next 30 days
Plan 30 days of posts for [business type] around these dates and events: [holidays, local events, launches, slow weeks]. Put it in a table: date, post idea, goal (sell / book / show personality / teach), and which photo to use.
Fix a caption that sounds like an ad
Rewrite this caption so it sounds like the owner talking, not an ad. Shorter sentences, one idea, one call to action:
[paste caption]
Part 03 · 5 prompts

Email and follow-ups

so nobody falls through the cracks
Sort today's inbox
Here are the emails I got today:
[paste them]
Sort them into four piles: answer today, can wait, hand to someone else, ignore. Then draft replies for the "answer today" pile.
Follow up with a lead
Someone asked about [service] on [date] and never booked. Write three short follow-up emails: one for tomorrow, one for next week, and a last friendly check-in two weeks out. No pressure, and make each one useful on its own.
Win back regulars
Write an email to customers who haven't been in for [number] weeks. Make it feel personal, remind them what they loved about [business name], and give them a reason to come back: [offer or news]. Under 120 words, with two subject line options.
Announce a price increase
Our prices on [services or products] are going up from [old price] to [new price] on [date]. Write a short, honest email that explains why in one or two sentences, thanks customers for sticking with us, and doesn't sound like an apology.
Write the newsletter from rough notes
Here are my rough notes for this month:
[paste notes]
Turn them into a short newsletter: a friendly opener, three quick sections, and one call to action at the end. Give me three subject lines.
Part 04 · 5 prompts

Scheduling and your team

this one's a big one
First-draft schedule
Here's everyone's availability for next week:
[paste availability]
We need [number] people on [shifts]. Make a first-draft schedule in a table. Flag any shift that isn't covered and anyone over [number] hours.
Split the tips
This week's tips: $[total]. Hours worked:
[name: hours, one per line]
Split the tips by hours worked. Show the math in a table and check that the total adds up to $[total] exactly. Round to the cent and tell me where any leftover penny went.
Check the timesheets
Here are this week's timesheets and the schedule:
[paste both]
Compare them and list anything that doesn't match: missed punches, long breaks, overtime, or shifts that ran past close.
Opening and closing checklists
Write an opening checklist and a closing checklist for a [business type]. Here's what we do now, in no particular order:
[paste your notes]
Put it in order, group it by area, and keep each line short enough to check off.
Plan a hard conversation
I need to talk to [role] about [the issue]. It's happened [how often]. Help me plan a 10-minute conversation: how to open, the one thing I need to say clearly, two questions to ask them, and how to end with a clear next step.
Part 05 · 5 prompts

Hiring and training

so you stop being the only one who knows
Write the job post
Write a job post for a [role] at [business name] in [city]. Pay: [pay]. Hours: [hours]. What the job is really like: [notes]. Make it honest and specific, not corporate, and end with how to apply.
Interview questions
Give me 8 interview questions for a [role] that show how someone handles a busy day, an upset customer and a slow shift. For each question, tell me what a good answer sounds like.
New hire's first week
Make a first-week plan for a new [role]. Day by day: what they learn, who shows them, and what they should be able to do on their own by Friday.
Turn your brain dump into a training doc
Here's how I do [task], typed out the messy way:
[paste notes]
Turn it into a step-by-step training doc a new person could follow without asking me. Add a short list of common mistakes at the end.
Answer the team's questions for you
Here are our policies and the questions my team asks me most:
[paste]
Write a one-page cheat sheet with short answers they can check before they come find me.
Part 06 · 5 prompts

Ordering, inventory and admin

so much of this can go
Reorder list from your sales
Here's last week's sales report and what we have on hand:
[paste both]
Make a reorder list in a table: item, how many we sold, how many we have, how many to order. Flag anything that's selling faster than usual.
Email a vendor
Write an email to [vendor] about [late order / wrong item / price change / new order]. Be friendly and clear, include [order number or details], and ask for [what you need] by [date].
Write a policy
Write our [cancellation / refund / late arrival / deposit] policy for a [business type]. Here's how we handle it now: [notes]. Keep it short, fair and clear enough that nobody can argue with it. Then give me a one-line version for the website.
Turn meeting notes into a to-do list
Here are my notes from [meeting]:
[paste notes]
Pull out every decision and every to-do. List each to-do with who's doing it and by when. If I didn't say who or when, mark it so I can fill it in.
Write a how-we-do-it doc
Write a simple step-by-step for how we [process, like taking a custom order or handling a return]. Here's what happens now: [notes]. Number the steps and note who's responsible for each one.
Part 07 · 5 prompts

Numbers and pricing

no more guessing
Weekly recap in plain English
Here's this week's sales report:
[paste or upload it]
Give me a plain-English recap: what sold, what didn't, how this week compares to last week, and one thing to do next week because of it. Five bullet points max.
What you really make per hour
Here's my service menu with prices, how long each one takes, and what supplies cost:
[paste]
Work out what I make per hour on each service after supplies. Show it in a table from highest to lowest and tell me which ones to raise or rethink.
Find the money leaks
Here are my expenses for the last 3 months:
[paste or upload]
Group them, flag anything that went up, anything I'm paying for twice, and subscriptions I might not use. Give me the top 3 to look at first.
Busy hours vs. slow hours
Here are my sales by day and hour:
[paste]
Show me my busiest and slowest times in a simple table. Then tell me if my staffing matches it and where I'm paying for hours that don't make money.
Friendly invoice reminder
Write a friendly reminder for an invoice that's [number] days late. Invoice [number] for $[amount], due [date]. Polite, short, and clear about how to pay: [payment link]. Then write a firmer second reminder for a week later.
Part 08 · 5 prompts

Customer experience and growth

grow what you already have
See your business like a first-timer
Pretend you're a first-time customer finding us online. Here's our website text and Google listing:
[paste]
What's confusing, what's missing, and what would make you not book? Then list the 3 fixes that would matter most.
Sell more to the customers you have
Here's what we sell and what customers usually buy together:
[paste]
Give me 5 easy ways to raise the average ticket without being pushy: add-ons, bundles, or what to mention at checkout. Write the one line my team can say for each.
Start a simple loyalty program
Design a simple loyalty program for a [business type] that's easy to run without a fancy app. Our average customer comes in [how often] and spends about $[amount]. Give me two options and what each one would cost me.
Ask for referrals
Write a short, natural way to ask happy customers for a referral: one line to say in person, one text, and one email. Our referral reward is [reward], or suggest one if I don't have one yet.
Pick what to fix first
Here's everything on my plate right now:
[paste your list]
Sort it into: makes money, saves time, or can wait. Then tell me the one thing to do this week that will free up the most time.
