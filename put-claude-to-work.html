Part 01

Why I made this

I spent 10+ years running cafés and shops: managing teams, building schedules, doing the ordering, posting on social at 11pm. Then I took a job where I had to learn AI fast, with no real training and nobody to ask.

I learned the basics online and was starting to get the hang of it. But my setup was a mess. Every chat started from zero and my files lived everywhere. I didn't know what GitHub was, and I wasn't touching code.

Then my boss learned about GitHub. Instead of showing it to me, he said, "Open up your Claude. Let me see if you're doing it wrong." It was just the two of us, but that's not how anyone wants to be shown something new.

Around the same time, a friend of mine had a really organized workflow. Seeing it, I started cleaning up my own setup to match: one place for everything about the work, instructions Claude follows every time, and asking for the finished thing instead of advice. Now it's organized, and it works the way it should.

This guide is that setup, written for business owners. You don't need to be techy, and you don't need GitHub or code for any of it. You need about an hour and the stuff you already know about your business.

Now I help small business owners set this up in their own businesses. If you'd rather do it together, book a free Strategy Session any time.

Chatbot vs. teammate

Most people use Claude the first way. All the time savings live in the second.

Using it like a chatbotUsing it like a teammate
Re-explain your business in every chatIt already knows your prices, hours, policies and voice
Ask for tips, then do the work yourselfAsk for the finished thing: the schedule, the post, the checklist
Copy and paste the answer into another appIt makes the doc, spreadsheet or file for you
Sounds like a robot wrote itSounds like you on a good day
One question at a timeHand off a whole task and check the result
AI can't fix a mess. It speeds up whatever's already there.

If your prices live in three places and your closing steps live in your head, start there. The setup below makes you write those things down once, and that alone saves time.

Part 04

The setup, in five steps

Do these in order. Steps 1 and 2 take under an hour and do most of the work. Anything that looks like a [highlighted blank] is where your own details go.

1

Tell Claude who you are

In Claude's settings, turn on memory and web search. Then find the spot that asks what Claude should know about you, and paste this in with your details. Now every chat starts knowing who it's talking to.

Paste into your profile
I own [business name], a [type of business] in [city]. I've run it for [X] years. Most of my customers are [who they are].
I'm busy. Give me short, practical answers I can use today, no fluff.
When you write as me, sound [3 words, like: warm, direct, a little funny].
If you need more info, ask me a question instead of guessing.
2

Build your Business Brain

A Project is a folder Claude reads before it answers. Make one called "Business Brain" and add these files. Rough notes are fine. A typed list in a doc is fine.

  • About us: what you sell, who buys it, what makes you different
  • Prices and hours: the real, current ones
  • Policies: returns, bookings, cancellations, time off
  • Our voice: 3 to 5 posts or emails you wrote that sound like you
  • Top questions: the 10 things customers ask you most

Then paste this into the project's instructions:

Project instructions
You're the operations assistant for [business name].
Before you answer, check the files in this project: About Us, Prices and Hours, Policies, Our Voice, Top Questions.
Always use our real prices and policies, write in our voice, and keep it short.
If something isn't in the files, ask me. Don't make it up.
When I ask for something I'll use (a schedule, a post, a checklist), make the finished thing, not advice about it.
good to knowChats inside a project share the project's files, not each other's conversations. If you want Claude to remember something every time, add it to a project file, not just a chat.
3

Make your first skill

A skill is a recipe card Claude follows every time a certain job comes up, so you stop typing the same instructions. Start with something you do every week. Review replies are a good first one.

The easy way: do the job once in a chat, get it how you like it, then say "turn what we just did into a skill." Or write one yourself. It's just a short text file you upload in Claude's settings under Skills.

Example skill: review replies
---
name: review-replies
description: Use when I paste a customer review from Google, Yelp or Facebook and need a reply.
---
# Review replies for [business name]
1. Thank them by first name if it's given.
2. Mention one specific thing they said.
3. If they had a problem, own it in one sentence, no excuses, and invite them to email [your email].
4. Keep it under 70 words.
5. Sign off as "[your name], owner".
Give me two versions: one short, one warmer.
tipThe "description" line is how Claude knows when to use the skill. Write it as "Use when…" and name the moment it should kick in.
4

Connect one app

A connector lets Claude look inside an app you already use, like Gmail, Google Calendar, Google Drive, Notion or Canva. Once it's connected, you can say "find the email from my linen vendor" or "what does my week look like?" and Claude goes and checks, so you stop copying and pasting things in.

How to add one
  1. In Claude, open Customize in the sidebar, then Connectors.
  2. Click Discover to browse, or search for the app by name.
  3. Open it and click Connect to Claude, then sign in to that app and approve access. It now shows as "Connected."
  4. In any chat, click the + in the message box, then Connectors, to turn it on or off for that chat.

The first time Claude uses a connector, it asks permission. Choose Allow once while you're getting comfortable, and Always allow once you trust how it's using it.

what's an MCP?You'll see "MCP" around connectors. It's the behind-the-scenes standard apps use to plug into Claude. You don't need to know how it works. If an app you use isn't in the directory, search the app's name plus "MCP" or "Claude connector" to see if it offers one.
  • Start with one app, the one you live in most.
  • Start with reading tasks: finding, summarizing, drafting.
  • Check anything before it gets sent or booked in your name.
5

Hand off a real task

This is where it clicks. Instead of asking a question, hand over a job the way you'd hand it to a good employee: what the job is, what done looks like, and what to use. Claude can come back with an actual doc, spreadsheet, checklist or slide deck you can open and edit.

The hand-off formula
The job: [what you need done]
Done looks like: [the finished thing: a spreadsheet, a one-page doc, 5 captions…]
Use: [my Business Brain files, this attachment, my calendar]
Before you start, ask me anything you need to know.

Part 06

Three plays, start to finish

Here's what a full hand-off looks like. Each one takes about 20 minutes the first time and less after that.

Fill your slowest day

For: a café, shop or studio with one day that always drags.

You give itLast month's sales by day and hour (an export from your register or POS), plus your Business Brain.
You ask"Find my slowest window. Suggest 3 promos that fit our prices and brand. Pick the best one and make everything I need to run it."
You get backThe promo, three captions, a sign for the counter, and a short email to your list.

one afternoon of work, done before lunch

Get yourself out of the schedule

For: anyone with a team who's still building the schedule at midnight.

You give itEveryone's availability, how many people each shift needs, and your rules for swaps and time off.
You ask"Make a first-draft schedule as a spreadsheet. Flag any shift I can't cover. Then write the message I'll send the team."
You get backA schedule you edit instead of build, a list of gaps, and a clear team message.

you go from builder to editor

Find where money is leaking

For: the owner who knows it's busy but isn't sure it's profitable.

You give itA sales export for the last 1 to 3 months and a list of your monthly costs.
You ask"Show my top and bottom sellers, what I need to sell each month to cover costs, and three things you'd look into. Show your math."
You get backA one-page summary you can check, plus a short list of what to cut, push or reprice.

check the math. always check the math.

Part 07

Mistakes I made (so you don't)

  • A new chat for everythingfix:Work inside your Business Brain project so it never starts from zero.
  • Taking the first answerfix:Tell it exactly what's off, the way you'd mark up an employee's draft: "Cut this to 3 sentences. No exclamation points. Say 'regulars,' not 'customers.' Mention we're closed Mondays." Vague notes get vague rewrites.
  • Asking for advice when I wanted the thingfix:Ask for the finished file. "Make the checklist," not "how should I make a checklist?"
  • Pasting in private stufffix:Never share passwords, card numbers or private customer details. Leave them out or use first names only.
  • Hitting send without readingfix:You're the editor. Your name is on it, so read everything before it goes out.
  • Files everywherefix:One home for your business docs. When something changes, update the project file once.
